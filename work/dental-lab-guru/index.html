Context

Dental Lab Guru is a product I build and operate for dental laboratory workflows. Owning a product makes the engineering feedback loop very direct: the work is not complete when a feature is merged, because release decisions, questions and the next change all belong to the same product.

Building around a real workflow

Workflow software succeeds when it helps people keep moving through their day. That means the data model, interface and integrations need to reflect how work is actually handed over, clarified and completed—not merely how a feature list is organised.

The approach is to make the next useful action obvious, leave room for changing operational needs and keep the system understandable enough to improve without turning every update into a rewrite.

What product ownership includes

  • Turning customer observations into product and technical decisions.
  • Balancing the delivery of new workflow support with the reliability of what people already use.
  • Making release and support choices with the product's real operating context in mind.

A separate path for product visitors. This page explains the engineering work. For product information, visit the public Dental Lab Guru website.

What this illustrates

Building a small software product is a useful test of end-to-end judgement. It brings product design, technical choices, deployment, customer feedback and ongoing operation into one responsibility.
